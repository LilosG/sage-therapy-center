Racing thoughts at 2am, a knot that won't leave your stomach before a meeting, a habit of assuming the worst before you have any evidence for it — anxiety shows up differently for everyone who walks into the Carlsbad office. Kristin Moorehead-Malley, LMFT, works with Carlsbad clients on what's actually driving it, not just the symptoms on the surface.

CBT tends to be a natural fit for anxiety specifically, since it gives you concrete ways to notice and interrupt the thought patterns that keep worry cycling. EMDR can come into play too, particularly when anxiety traces back to something specific rather than feeling free-floating. Which approach fits, and when, gets figured out together rather than decided before a first conversation.

Some Carlsbad clients come in with anxiety that's been building for years and finally hit a point where it's affecting sleep, work, or relationships. Others notice it flaring around a specific stretch — a job change, a health scare, becoming a parent — and want support before it settles into a longer-term pattern. Both are reasonable starting points, and neither requires a diagnosis in hand before reaching out.

Because the office sits right on Avenida Encinas, Carlsbad clients from La Costa, Olde Carlsbad, Bressi Ranch, Aviara, and Calavera Hills can usually fit sessions around a work schedule without losing half a day to travel. That matters more than it sounds for anxiety work specifically, since consistency tends to move things forward faster than sessions squeezed in sporadically.

Anxiety therapy here isn't a standalone track — it's individual therapy with anxiety as the focus, so if it turns out grief, a relationship pattern, or something else is tangled up in what's driving the anxiety, the sessions can widen to address that too, rather than staying narrowly on one label.

If what you're carrying feels like it's become unmanageable on your own, or physical symptoms like chest tightness or panic are part of it, that's worth naming when you reach out — sessions can address urgent-feeling anxiety, though ongoing crisis symptoms should also involve your physician or, if it's an emergency, 911 or a crisis line.

Request a session to talk through what's going on and whether anxiety-focused individual therapy is a fit.
